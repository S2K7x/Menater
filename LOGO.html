MENATER · Identity · Six routes, tested against the literature

The mark

Decided: Route 2, the Register. It is in the application — header and sign-in lockups, and the browser tab, which repaints itself in whichever of the six themes is chosen. The record of the other five is kept below, because a rejected option is part of the decision.

Six candidates for the symbol that sits beside the wordmark. Each is drawn on the same 32 unit grid, shown at the four sizes that decide it, and scored against what the research actually says — not against taste. The wordmark is the logo; the mark earns the right to travel alone only once people recognise it.

What the evidence constrains

ShapeAngular, not circular. Circular logos activate softness; angular ones activate hardness and durability, and read as more premium.
DescriptivenessDescribe the method, never the threat. Descriptive logos raise brand evaluations — but the effect reverses for categories carrying negative associations. Security is one.
RecognitionNatural, harmonious, moderately elaborate. 195 logos, 13 characteristics. Pure geometric abstraction scores worse on true recognition and worse on false recognition.
SymmetrySymmetric here. Asymmetry raises perceived excitement and pays only for brands whose personality is excitement. MENATER's is competence and restraint.
Solo useNot yet. Only three icons are universally understood — home, print, search. Everything else needs the word beside it.
PlacementTop left, clickable. 89% better brand recall than on the right; 6× more likely to get someone home in one click than centred.
Route 1

Frame

Runner-up

Registration marks around something held in view. It describes the act the name means — מנטר, watching, in the present — and it describes it without drawing a threat, which is the one thing the descriptiveness research says to avoid here. Four-fold symmetric, entirely angular, and natural in Henderson & Cote's sense: crop marks and viewfinders are things people have seen. The centre block is the observed thing; the frame never claims to know what it is.

Route 2

Register

Chosen · shipped

Five ticks, and the middle one is a stub rather than a full bar. It is the product's first principle drawn literally: a missing value keeps its place instead of being omitted. Absence sits at the centre, so the mark stays symmetric — the gap is the composition, not a defect in it. Reads as a scale, a signal, a row of records; angular throughout; and it is the only route here that a viewer can be told the meaning of once and never forget.

Route 3

Prompt

Strong, with one risk

A cursor held between two brackets. The name is a verb in the present tense, and a cursor is the one glyph that means right now, and waiting — which is also the console's only accent-bearing state, needs human. Symmetric, angular, and maximally natural for this audience. The risk is category sameness: brackets and cursors are the most-used shorthand in developer tooling, so it identifies the field faster than it identifies the product.

Route 4

Cut corner

No longer recommended

The incumbent, kept here because a rejected option is part of the decision. A solid square with one corner removed — the missing field, drawn. It is angular, it survives 16 px better than anything else here, and it is the simplest to reproduce. But it is asymmetric, and asymmetry only pays for brands whose personality is excitement; MENATER's is competence. And on its own it is pure geometry — it depicts nothing, which is the weakest position for a brand nobody has met yet. Route 2 keeps the same idea and fixes both problems.

Route 5

Halves

Right idea, wrong scale

The product's actual structure: one triangle solid for what has happened — the SOC, where an alert is already an incident — and one drawn in outline for what is about to, the flaw still in the code. It is the only route that says there are two halves, and it is the truest description of the thing. It also loses its whole point at 16 px, where the outline half fills in and the mark collapses to a square. A logo that only works large is a logo that does not work.

Route 6

Chain

Precise, and easily misread

Three links of the hash-chained audit log, the last one still open — the record is written, and the present entry is not closed yet. Angular, symmetric in its rhythm, and descriptive of the mechanism the product is proudest of. Two costs: a chain of blocks reads as "blockchain" to a large share of the audience, which is an association nobody chose; and the open link's 1 px stroke is the first thing to disappear at favicon size.

Constant

The wordmark does not change

Whichever mark wins, MENATER set in Archivo Black, uppercase, tracking −0.01em, is the logo. It is legible at every size, it needs no explanation, and it is the only element that will be recognised before the brand is known. Clear space is the cap-height of the M on all four sides; minimum width for the lockup is 88 px, and below that the mark goes alone.

MENATER

מנטר appears once, small, as an origin note — on an About screen, the brand page, the repository README. It is a signature, never part of the lockup: it would be unreadable to most of the audience, and Hebrew is right-to-left, which breaks any horizontal lockup it is dropped into.

Refused

What this mark will never be

Not a matter of taste. Descriptive logos help — except where the category carries negative associations, where the effect reverses. Security is exactly such a category, so drawing the threat makes the logo descriptive in the direction that costs you.
